04 / How I evaluate a product

Every product becomes
a case worth investigating.

SHERLOCK is my product-investigation lens. Hover, focus or tap a card to see the question it helps me answer.

SSignal

What symptom, change or opportunity made this problem worth investigating now?

HHow do we know?

Which qualitative and quantitative evidence proves the signal is real?

EExamine the system

How do users, journeys, technology, business and operations affect one another?

RRoot cause

What underlying mechanism—not just the visible symptom—is creating the problem?

LList hypotheses

What competing, testable explanations could account for what we observed?

OOrder investigation

Which test gives the highest information gain for the least risk and effort?

CCreate interventions

Which UI, non-UI, operational or strategic response best addresses the cause?

KKnow it worked

Which metrics, experiments and guardrails prove the intervention created value?

Nexa · Fintech case study

Confidence before payment

Problem

UPI makes transactions fast, but a balance alone does not tell people what they can safely spend after upcoming commitments.

Product move

Reframe the balance as Safe-to-Spend and connect subscriptions, shared finance, scan-to-pay and contextual AI guidance around that decision.

Design principle

Show the consequence before commitment. Trust should be built into the product model, not added as reassurance copy.

Contribution

Research synthesis, product strategy, flows, UX psychology, interface design, prototyping and system definition.

Booking.com · Travel redesign

One trip, not five bookings

Problem

Travellers experience one journey, while the product splits discovery, stays, experiences, checkout and post-booking into disconnected decisions.

Product move

Create a personalised, journey-aware system for business travellers and luxury explorers that preserves context across the trip.

Key interactions

Destination previews, enhanced search, curated experiences, responsive patterns, hover feedback and animated continuity.

Contribution

Journey mapping, information architecture, interaction design, responsive design system, prototyping and usability reasoning.

Soft · Mental-health product

Support without pressure

Problem

Mental-health tools can feel clinical, demanding or unsafe at exactly the moment a person needs low-friction support.

Direction

A soft visual and conversational system that prioritises emotional safety, privacy, calm check-ins and progressive disclosure.

Principle

Never turn care into performance. The product supports reflection without guilt, streak pressure or visual overload.

Contribution

Research framing, emotional journey, information architecture, interface direction, illustration and interaction concepts.

Onepiece · Portfolio system

Identity through behavior

Problem

Many designer portfolios look like polished marketing sites but do not demonstrate interaction thinking or a repeatable system.

System

A light editorial hierarchy, reusable case and project structures, semantic tokens and a 70×70 interactive mascot.

Mascot logic

Awake on entry, follows pointer and scroll, works on project focus, sleeps after inactivity and sends a plane when contact opens.

Contribution

Workflow, IA, wireframes, visual system, interaction specification, accessible implementation and browser QA.

Echo · AI systems case study

One intent, coordinated action

Problem

Everyday plans are scattered across calendars, websites, subscriptions and approvals. Most assistants answer questions but leave the coordination to the user.

Product move

Translate a short intent into parallel specialist tasks, combine their evidence, detect conflicts and pause irreversible actions behind a clear approval gate.

System

Calendar, cinema, subscription and booking adapters share a deterministic plan, evidence model and persistent run history.

Contribution

Product architecture, orchestration model, conflict UX, approval design, evaluation scenarios and implementation direction.

Spine · Research system

Intervene only after understanding

Problem

Teams often jump from a visible symptom to a familiar solution without separating competing explanations or checking the mechanism underneath.

Framework

Spine maps evidence through mechanisms, links, leaks and inversions, then runs a six-pass audit before recommending intervention.

Decision discipline

Each hypothesis receives explicit confidence, supporting and contradicting evidence, missing evidence and predicted observations.

Contribution

61-principle library, 12 mechanisms, audit engine, five-gate Forge, severity instruments and a compounding learning loop.
