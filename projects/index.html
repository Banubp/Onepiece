01 / Overview

Community products fail when discovery and participation live in different places.

People may discover an interest through social media, find a workshop on an event platform, join a club through messaging and buy supplies somewhere else. Hobby Ghar brings those related actions into one interest-led system.

Project snapshot

What the work needed to solve.

Problem

Fragmented participation

The journey from “I want to try this” to “I belong here” is distributed across disconnected tools and communities.

Product goal

Shorten the distance to action

Help people move from interest discovery to a relevant event, club or shop without rebuilding context at each step.

My responsibility

Product and UI/UX design

Structure the experience, clarify the main journeys and translate the community model into responsive interface patterns.

Boundary

Evidence before theatre

Final research artifacts, collaboration details and performance outcomes will be added only after their sources are confirmed.

02 / Product model

One interest.
Four connected jobs.

The architecture is organised around what someone wants to do next—not around separate product departments.

01Discover

Find a hobby or nearby activity that feels approachable.

02Participate

Understand the event, commitment and people involved.

03Belong

Return to a club, conversation or recurring routine.

04Equip

Find relevant local shops, makers and materials.

03 / Selected decisions

The product judgment—not a generic process timeline.

Decision 01

Lead with interests, not feature categories.

Events, clubs and shops are system types. “Pottery”, “cycling” or “photography” are how people recognise what they care about. Interest becomes the persistent container across discovery.

AVOIDEventsClubsShops

The user repeatedly chooses the system before the subject.

CHOSENPotteryEvent · Club · Shop

The subject carries context into every next action.

Decision 02

Make commitment visible before joining.

A community action needs more than a persuasive image. Time, distance, skill level, group size and what to bring reduce uncertainty before someone commits.

Decision 03

Treat shops as part of participation.

Products become more relevant when connected to a real activity. The store surface should explain why an item matters to the hobby instead of behaving like an unrelated marketplace.

05 / System and craft

A calm system for a lively community.

Hierarchy

One dominant action per surface; contextual details remain available without competing.

Content

Plain verbs—discover, join, save and visit—describe outcomes rather than system behaviour.

Responsive

Cards become a single readable journey on narrow screens; no essential information depends on hover.

Accessibility

Visible focus, native controls, labelled actions, restrained motion and sufficient target sizes.

06 / Validation boundary

What can be claimed today.

The portfolio can truthfully show the product model, interface reasoning and Banu’s product-design role. It should not yet claim user-research sample sizes, conversion improvements or business outcomes without the original evidence.

07 / Reflection

Designing belonging means reducing the uncertainty around the first step.

The strongest product opportunity is not simply placing events, clubs and shops on one screen. It is carrying a person’s interest and confidence across those systems so every next action feels connected.
