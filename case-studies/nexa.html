01

Problem and opportunity

Why a fast payment can still leave a person financially uncertain.

Problem framing

A payment confirms that ₹2,500 moved, but rarely explains whether bills, savings and goals are still protected.

Indian users often divide financial tasks across UPI apps, bank apps, cards, investments and informal notes. The transaction is fast; understanding the consequence requires manual reconstruction.

The opportunity was not to build another feature-heavy super app. It was to connect payments with a simple, trustworthy picture of what the user can safely do next.

How might we turn transactions people already make into useful financial clarity—without slowing UPI down?Design challenge
User need

Confidence after action

Understand the consequence of a payment immediately.

Product goal

Meaningful return value

Create a reason to return after the payment is complete.

02

Research and analysis

What was known, what remained uncertain and how I planned to test it.

Evidence before interface

Because Nexa is an independent concept, I separated observed market patterns from assumptions requiring primary research.

Research activities

  • Competitor review of PhonePe, Google Pay, Paytm and Monzo
  • Journey analysis from salary receipt to daily spending and saving
  • Planned contextual interviews around real payment decisions
  • Seven-day diary study to capture uncertainty in context
  • Card sorting and concept comprehension tests

Key analysis

UPI products are strong at reach and payment completion. Digital banks are stronger at budgeting clarity, but assume a single-bank relationship that does not match India’s multi-app, multi-account behaviour.

07

Validation plan

How the concept would be tested without inventing outcomes.

Prototype success criteria

The product succeeds only if people understand the guidance and retain control.

ComprehensionUsers correctly explain payment state and Safe to Spend inputs.
CorrectionWrong assumptions are found and fixed without support.
Meaningful actionA payment leads to a relevant budget, goal or insight action.
TrustGuidance is useful, neutral and appropriately confident.
No fabricated business impact

Monthly Financially Active Users is a proposed north-star. It remains a hypothesis until a real prototype is tested and the product is shipped.

08

Reflection

What changed in my thinking and what comes next.

Key learning

The strongest decision was choosing financial clarity—not visual similarity to Monzo—as the organising principle.

India is UPI-first, multi-bank, QR-heavy and influenced by family financial behaviour. Copying a UK digital bank would have missed the operating context. The next step is primary research focused on the moment after payment and whether users trust a calculated “safe” amount.
