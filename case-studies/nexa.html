Nexa · Self-initiated 0→1 fintech concept

Beyond payments.

Reimagining everyday money management for India around one promise: clarity after every transaction.

Nexa connects UPI payments, budgeting, savings, subscriptions and responsible guidance without becoming another cluttered financial super app.

The speed of UPI. The clarity of a digital bank.

01

The opportunity

Problem framing

Payments became effortless. Money management did not.

A payment app can confirm that ₹2,500 moved, but rarely explains whether bills are still covered or what that transaction changed.

Indian users often divide financial tasks across UPI apps, bank apps, credit-card portals, investment platforms and informal notes. The transaction is fast; the understanding around it remains fragmented.

The design challenge became: How might we help people understand and organise their finances through transactions they already make—without slowing UPI down?

What is happening with my money, and what should I do next?The organising question behind Nexa
01 · Speed

Preserve habit

Scanning, paying and confirming must remain familiar and immediate.

02 · Clarity

Explain consequence

Every transaction should update a comprehensible financial picture.

03 · Trust

Keep control visible

Guidance must show its source, confidence and correction path.

02

Research and evidence

Evidence before interface

Study the riskiest assumptions.

Because this is an independent concept, I did not present fictional interviews as validated research.

I combined secondary research and competitor benchmarking with a primary-research plan for the questions that still require real users: how people split financial tasks, when uncertainty appears, why budgeting tools are abandoned and what makes automated guidance feel trustworthy.

Will users value financial clarity enough to return after the payment is complete?

03

User and journey

Primary user · Ananya Rao

High digital confidence. Moderate financial confidence.

Ananya uses UPI several times a day, has two bank accounts, subscriptions and a travel goal—but saves whatever remains at month-end.

Functional job

Reduce manual tracking

Show what is safe to use across connected money without a spreadsheet.

Emotional job

Replace anxiety with control

Help the user understand changes without judgemental language.

04

Product decisions

From principles to interface

Design around four questions.

The home screen is deliberately limited: how much do I have, what can I safely spend, what needs attention and what can I do now?

Decision 01

Safe to Spend

Available balance minus upcoming bills, protected savings and a personal buffer—always visible and editable.

Decision 02

Spaces

Use natural mental accounting for bills, travel, emergencies and family goals.

Decision 03

Smart activity

Turn transaction history into a searchable timeline with corrections, receipts, splits and recurring-charge detection.

Decision 04

Explainable coach

Every insight shows what was noticed, the evidence used, confidence and how the user can correct it.

05

Validation and outcome

Concept measurement plan

Measure understanding, not decoration.

No shipped-product impact is claimed. These are the signals I would use to prove or reject the concept.

01Payment-state comprehension
02Safe-to-Spend correction rate
03Meaningful post-payment actions
04Support satisfaction and trust

The proposed north-star is Monthly Financially Active Users: people who complete a payment and then perform at least one meaningful money-management action, such as reviewing an insight, updating a budget or contributing to a goal.

The project began as an Indian interpretation of a modern digital bank. The design process showed why copying Monzo would fail: India is UPI-first, multi-bank, QR-heavy, family-influenced and varied in device, language and connectivity conditions.

The most important decision was not a colour or card style. It was choosing financial clarity as the product’s organising principle.Reflection
