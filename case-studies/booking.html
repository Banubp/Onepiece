Booking.com · Strategic redesign

One trip, not five bookings.

A connected travel experience that preserves Booking.com’s reliable core while carrying trip context from discovery to support.

RoleLead Product Designer
Timeline4 weeks
PlatformResponsive web
StatusStrategic concept

A successful booking engine can still create a fragmented journey.

The redesign adds continuity for efficient business travel and premium exploration without weakening the familiar path for users who already know what they need.

My SHERLOCK processSignal → evidence → intervention → proof

S

Signal

The opportunity behind a proven transaction engine.

Observed tension

Travellers experience one journey; the product often presents discovery, stays, experiences, checkout and support as separate decisions.

How might Booking.com grow from a transaction platform into a travel relationship without making booking slower?Investigation question
H

How do we know?

Evidence that distinguishes two travel contexts.

Secondary research + benchmarking

The same traveller can want absolute efficiency on Monday and emotional discovery on Saturday.

Business travel

Location, policy, invoice readiness and rebooking carry more value than inspiration.

Premium exploration

Atmosphere, neighbourhood and trip fit matter more than a long amenity list.

Evidence gap

Primary testing must verify whether continuity reduces repeated decisions without lowering core conversion.

E

Examine the system

The full journey, stakeholders and guardrails.

Journey architecture

Destination, dates, intent and constraints should travel with the user—not reset at each purchase.

R

Root cause

Why strong local flows still feel disconnected.

Causal framing

The product optimises categories and transactions; the traveller organises around a trip.

Root cause

Context resets

Intent and constraints are repeatedly reconstructed across separate funnels.

Implication

The journey becomes the container

Bookings, changes, experiences and support inherit one trip context.
