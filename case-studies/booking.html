Booking.com · Strategic redesign

One trip, not five bookings.

A connected travel experience that preserves Booking.com’s reliable core while carrying context from discovery to support.

RoleLead Product Designer
Timeline4 weeks
PlatformResponsive web
StatusStrategic concept

Booking the stay is one task. Managing the journey is the real job.

This concept explores how Booking.com could preserve its efficient accommodation funnel while carrying traveller intent, constraints and support needs across the complete trip.

ProblemResearchUsersJourneyProcessSolutionImpactReflection
01

Problem and opportunity

A high-performing booking funnel can still make the traveller rebuild context at every stage.

From transaction efficiency to journey continuity

Search, comparison, checkout and post-booking support each work, but the traveller’s purpose and constraints do not consistently travel with them.

A business traveller may repeatedly verify location, cancellation, invoice and check-in details. A premium explorer may move between inspiration and literal filters without a clear explanation of why one property fits better than another.

The strategic opportunity is to connect those decisions around a persistent trip context while protecting Booking.com’s strongest asset: a familiar, dependable path to booking.

How might Booking.com carry traveller intent and critical constraints across the journey without weakening speed, price clarity or conversion?Problem statement
02

Research and analysis

Examining where traveller context is created, lost and reconstructed across the existing journey.

Product audit + competitive review

This strategic concept is grounded in secondary research and interface analysis; behavioural modes are hypotheses to validate, not claimed interview findings.

Research activities

  • Audited information continuity from discovery through post-booking
  • Reviewed search, property, checkout and trip-management heuristics
  • Compared Airbnb, Expedia and premium travel discovery patterns
  • Mapped recurring constraints in business and high-consideration travel
  • Inspected responsive, keyboard and price-policy clarity at critical steps

Synthesis

Two modes emerged as useful design hypotheses: task-led travel values predictability and speed; exploratory travel values confidence in fit. Both need continuity, but not the same entry experience.

03

Users and jobs

Two contextual modes instead of one fictional “average traveller.”

Primary hypothesis · Task-led business traveller

MK

Marcus King

Proto-persona · Consultant · Frequent traveller

Core job

Book a compliant, convenient stay quickly and retrieve every operational detail without searching again.

Decision criteria

  • Location and journey time
  • Flexible cancellation
  • Invoice and check-in certainty

Likely friction

Repeated filters, buried policies and trip information distributed across product surfaces and emails.

Successful outcome

A dependable booking that creates no avoidable follow-up administration.

Working hypothesis: surface anything that could disrupt the trip before commitment.

Secondary hypothesis · Exploratory premium traveller

Emotional job

Feel confident in the fit

Understand the atmosphere, neighbourhood and reason a stay suits this particular trip.

Functional job

Reduce comparison effort

See a smaller, well-explained set of meaningful alternatives rather than a larger catalogue.

07

Measurement strategy

How the concept would be evaluated without presenting proposed outcomes as real impact.

Success criteria

The first requirement is protecting core booking performance; continuity and growth only matter if travellers can still complete critical tasks confidently.

Task performanceSearch-to-booking completion, time on critical tasks and error recovery.
Decision qualityComparison effort, confidence and understanding of price and policy.
Journey continuityUse of saved trip context and successful post-booking retrieval.
Business signalRepeat use, premium booking value, satisfaction and relevant cross-category adoption.
Proposed measures, not shipped results

Evaluation would begin with moderated task-based research and accessibility checks, followed by controlled comparison of the existing and proposed discovery paths.

08

Reflection

The strongest strategic learning from the redesign.

Key learning

The opportunity was not “more travel features.” It was making the relationship between existing capabilities feel intentional.

A connected journey must earn its place by reducing repeated decisions. The next step would test the business and explorer variants separately, then measure whether a shared journey workspace transfers across both without creating a second navigation system.
