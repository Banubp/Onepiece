Booking.com · Self-initiated strategic redesign

Beyond transactions.

Reframing a proven booking platform as a connected travel ecosystem without weakening speed or reliability.

The redesign adds a discovery and continuity layer for two valuable contexts: efficient business travel and premium exploration.

RoleLead Product Designer
Timeline4 weeks
PlatformResponsive web
StatusStrategic concept

One journey, not five disconnected bookings.

Journey strategy · IA · Responsive UI
01

The opportunity

Problem framing

A successful booking engine can still create a fragmented trip.

Travellers experience one journey; the product often presents discovery, stays, experiences, checkout and post-booking as separate decisions.

Booking.com’s strength is dependable accommodation search at scale. The opportunity is not to replace that foundation, but to add continuity around it: remember the trip context, reduce repeated decisions and connect relevant experiences before and after the stay.

The central product question was: How might Booking.com grow from a transaction platform into a travel relationship without making the core booking flow slower?

User tension

Decision fatigue

Too many equivalent options create comparison loops instead of confidence.

Business tension

One-off utility

Users often leave after completing a booking, limiting the relationship.

Design tension

Premium without exclusion

Elevate discovery while keeping the familiar, accessible booking path.

02

Research synthesis

Secondary research and benchmarking

Two contexts, different definitions of value.

The same traveller can want absolute efficiency on Monday and emotional discovery on Saturday.

Context A · Business

Predictability wins

Location, cancellation policy, invoice readiness, check-in time and fast rebooking carry more value than inspiration.

Context B · Explorer

Confidence through curation

Atmosphere, neighbourhood, experiences and trip fit matter more than a long feature checklist.

03

Journey architecture

Discovery to post-booking

Preserve context across the whole trip.

Instead of treating each purchase as a new funnel, the system carries destination, dates, intent and constraints forward.

  1. DiscoverFind a directionEditorial stories and curated collections
  2. SearchShape the tripDates, people, purpose and constraints
  3. ExploreCompare confidentlyFit, trade-offs and neighbourhood context
  4. BookCommit clearlyPrice, policy and next steps
  5. ManageContinue the journeyStay, experiences and support together

Information architecture

  • Stays as the dependable booking core
  • Journeys as the persistent trip workspace
  • Escapes as curated multi-part recommendations
  • Experiences connected to time and place

Guardrail

Discovery can enrich the journey, but never block a user who already knows exactly what they need.

04

Design decisions

Two modes, one system

Make relevance visible before persuasion.

Decision 01

Contextual hero

Business entry prioritises dates and location; explorer entry leads with place and possibility.

Decision 02

Curated collections

Group stays by meaningful trip intent rather than arbitrary promotional labels.

Decision 03

Progressive property detail

Surface fit and essential trade-offs first; deeper facilities remain available.

Decision 04

Journey continuity

Keep bookings, changes, experiences and support inside one trip context.

Checkout · familiar structure, stronger price and policy clarity

05

Validation and outcome

Measurement framework

Prove continuity without harming booking efficiency.

Because this is a concept redesign, these are proposed measures—not claimed business results.

01Search-to-booking completion
02Decision time and comparison depth
03Cross-category journey adoption
04Repeat booking and trip return rate

A staged validation plan would first test comprehension and task completion for business travel, then test whether curated discovery improves confidence without lowering conversion. Accessibility checks include keyboard navigation, readable type, responsive reflow and status communication beyond colour.

The key learning was that the redesign did not need to make Booking.com feel like a different company. It needed to make the relationship between its strongest capabilities feel intentional.

The product opportunity is not “more travel features.” It is one coherent journey that remembers why the user came.Reflection
