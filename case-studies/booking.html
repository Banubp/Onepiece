01

Problem and opportunity

Why a category-leading platform can still lose the relationship after checkout.

Problem framing

Travellers experience one journey; the product often presents discovery, stays, experiences, checkout and post-booking as separate decisions.

Booking.com is exceptionally strong at dependable accommodation search and conversion. The redesign opportunity was not to replace that engine, but to connect the moments around it.

The business tension is equally important: a platform used for one transaction has fewer opportunities to build preference, repeat use and cross-category value.

How might Booking.com become a travel relationship without adding friction to its proven booking path?Design challenge
02

Research and competitive analysis

Studying traveller context, category expectations and the existing product.

Secondary research + heuristic review

The same traveller can prioritise absolute efficiency on Monday and emotional discovery on Saturday.

Methods

  • Existing journey and information-architecture audit
  • Competitor benchmarking across Airbnb, Expedia and premium travel platforms
  • Heuristic review of search, property, checkout and post-booking
  • Review of business-travel and luxury-explorer needs
  • Responsive and accessibility inspection of critical tasks

Synthesis

Business travellers optimise for predictability: location, policy, invoice and check-in. Explorers optimise for confidence in fit: atmosphere, neighbourhood and experience.

07

Validation and intended impact

Proving continuity without weakening booking performance.

Measurement plan

The first guardrail is core booking parity; growth measures come only after task performance holds.

CompletionSearch-to-booking completion and critical-task success.
Decision effortTime, comparison depth and confidence at selection.
ContinuityJourney workspace use and post-booking return.
Business valuePremium bookings, repeat use, ABV, CSAT and cross-category engagement.
Strategic concept

These are intended measures, not claimed shipped results. Testing would begin with moderated usability, responsive keyboard checks and an A/B concept test for curated discovery.

08

Reflection

The strongest strategic learning from the redesign.

Key learning

The opportunity was not “more travel features.” It was making the relationship between existing capabilities feel intentional.

A connected journey must earn its place by reducing repeated decisions. The next step would test the business and explorer variants separately, then measure whether a shared journey workspace transfers across both without creating a second navigation system.
